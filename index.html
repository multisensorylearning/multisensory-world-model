Multisensory Continual Learning Adapting Pretrained Visuomotor Policies to Force

Anonymous Authors

We show that pretrained visuomotor policies can learn to leverage new modalities in finetuning. Models trained with MuSe leverage limited force data to improve in contact-rich tasks seen during finetuning, in addition to improving performance on original tasks with no additional data.

Abstract

Robot manipulation often depends on sensory data beyond vision, especially in contact-rich tasks where force, tactile, or audio feedback reveals interaction states not directly visible from images. Yet these modalities are hardware- and task-specific, and large multisensory datasets remain scarce, making it impractical to pretrain policies with every sensor they may encounter.

We study multisensory continual learning: adapting a pretrained robot policy to new tasks with newly introduced modalities while preserving performance under the original sensor suite. We propose MuSe, which integrates limited multisensory data into pretrained vision-action policies using token-level multi-stage fusion, multisensory future prediction, and episodic recall on pretraining data. We find that MuSe:

  • Improves contact-rich finetuning tasks that benefit from force feedback.
  • Predicts force on pretraining tasks where force supervision was never used.
  • Improves performance on original pretraining tasks after multisensory finetuning.

Method

Multi-stage fusion

Force histories are encoded into the same token space as images, proprioception, language, and actions for early transformer-level interaction, then injected again through late cross-attention adapters.

Multisensory future prediction

The model predicts future actions, future visual observations, and future F/T signals, encouraging a shared representation grounded in both visual dynamics and contact.

Episodic recall

Finetuning mixes new multisensory data with the original pretraining data. Missing force inputs are masked, preserving old skills while adding the new modality.

Dataset and Key Results

+35% forward contact-rich transfer

On vase wiping and finetuning peg insertion, MuSe improves from 46.7% to 81.7% success over the No F/T input baseline.

+20% improved backward transfer

On original contact-rich tasks, MuSe improves from 55.0% to 75.0% success over the pretrained vision-only policy.

Cross-Modal Generalization

MuSe predicts force where force was never supervised.

After learning from limited multisensory finetuning data, MuSe can predict F/T traces on pretraining tasks that only used vision-action supervision.

  • Shared multisensory representation: F/T prediction on unlabeled pretraining tasks suggests the model learns a shared multisensory representation that transfers beyond the finetuning distribution.
  • Adaptive compliance at runtime: predicted future F/T provides a contact profile that can set compliance targets during deployment.

Why F/T?

Force-torque sensing exposes physical interactions that images cannot capture

MuSe uses F/T in two complementary ways. As an input, force histories tell the policy when contact has started, whether the robot is pushing too hard, and when a peg has jammed. As an output, predicted future F/T gives the controller an expected contact profile that can be converted into adaptive compliance targets, so the robot can stay stiff in free space and become compliant when contact is anticipated.

We find that both F/T input and output is essential for contact-rich tasks. Wiping needs sufficient normal force to erase marks without hitting safety limits, while peg insertion needs to leverage force for recovery from friction and jams rather than blindly pushing.
